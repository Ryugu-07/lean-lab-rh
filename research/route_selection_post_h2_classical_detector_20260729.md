## Source lock

The historical anchor is A. M. Turing,
[*Some Calculations of the Riemann Zeta-Function*](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-3.1.99)
(1953). Turing's method certifies that a purported list of zeros is complete by comparing the
observed list with an analytically controlled zero count.

Two modern source checks fix the claim boundary:

- Dave Platt and Tim Trudgian,
  [*The Riemann hypothesis is true up to 3 times 10^12*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09765),
  prove that every zeta zero through their certified height lies on the critical line.
- Jan Buthe,
  [*A method for proving the completeness of a list of zeros of certain
  L-functions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6704), explicitly separates candidate-zero
  computation from the completeness theorem and compares its Weil-Barner method with Turing's
  count method.

The campaign formalizes the completeness consumer, not interval arithmetic, an average bound for
`S(T)`, or a new finite-height record.
