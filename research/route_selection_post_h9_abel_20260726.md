## Why this is the current omission target

Groskin's Theorem 2.5 states that the cutoff-free finite matrix quadratic is exactly the
multiplicity-bearing zero sum of the induced test. The project has separately compiled almost
all objects on both sides, but not the theorem that joins them. Formalizing that join tests a
real historical/spectral claim and can expose a missed normalization, regularity shortcut, or
analytic lemma. It does not spend another loop improving the numerical archimedean tail budget.

The exact first analytic risk is already visible. The generic selected-height contour theorem
can consume differentiability, reflection symmetry, absolute zero summability, and vanishing of
the selected top horizontal integral. The dictionary supplies the first three. Its test has only
inverse-square horizontal-strip decay, while the project's current selected-height bound for
`logDeriv riemannXi` grows like the fourth power of the height scale. Those two estimates do not
prove the top edge tends to zero.

A successful proof therefore needs either:

1. a sharper source-valid selected-height logarithmic-derivative estimate;
2. a smoothing/approximation proof with convergence on the zero, pole, prime, and archimedean
   sides; or
3. another exact Guinand--Weil theorem at the source's admissibility regularity.
